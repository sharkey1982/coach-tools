Floor Coaching Toolkit

A practical challenge, support and constraint-led learning bank for recreational floor sessions.

Core idea: a gymnast completing a skill is not the end of the activity. Change the problem — shape, accuracy, control, time, entry, exit, linking, partner work or creativity. This gives capable gymnasts somewhere to go next without requiring a new acrobatic skill every time.

How to use it: start by asking "Show me your best…". Observe before correcting. If the skill needs support, change the task or environment with a prop or constraint. If the gymnast already owns the skill, give a challenge rather than simply moving them to a harder acrobatic skill.

Attempt→ Identify one limitation→ Change the task→ Retry→ Remove the aid and retest

● Coaching Language to Build Independence

Start"Show me your best one."
Then"Your challenge is…"
For capable gymnasts"Now make it better."

The aim is to move away from children needing a coach to supply the next exercise. They should learn that completing a skill opens another challenge: improve its quality, hit a target, control it, link it, perform it with a partner or create a variation. Coach attention is most valuable when a gymnast is stuck, has achieved a meaningful breakthrough, or needs technical/safety input.

● Session Design Implications

Younger groups

Fewer simultaneous choices. Use a common theme (for example Rolls, Upside Down or Sideways), offer several safe versions within it, then regroup and change theme.

Middle / older groups

Zones can contain visible challenge levels. Gymnasts work more independently while coaches anchor on technical work. Levels should offer depth rather than merely escalating to harder acrobatics.

Safety principle

Independent tasks must be suitable to perform imperfectly while the coach is looking elsewhere. New, assisted or higher-risk acrobatic skills and apparatus progressions remain coached/supervised and should follow the venue's and governing body's procedures.
