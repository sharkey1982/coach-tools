Number Lines

Position, distance and ordering · Y3–Y6

Bar models show part-whole relationships (sharing, ratio, fractions of an amount). Number lines show position and distance — where a number sits, how far apart two numbers are, and which of several numbers is bigger. Use a number line whenever a question is really about "where" or "how far", even if it doesn't look like it at first: temperature changes, rounding, time durations, and ordering fractions/decimals/percentages together are all distance problems in disguise.

45 is the halfway point between 40 and 50. 47 is past halfway, so it's closer to 50: 47 rounds up to 50.

Question 6

A train leaves at 09:45 and arrives at 11:20. How long is the journey?

Break the journey into easy chunks: up to 10:00, then up to 11:00, then the rest.

09:45 → 10:00 is 15 min. 10:00 → 11:00 is 60 min. 11:00 → 11:20 is 20 min. Total: 15 + 60 + 20 = 95 min = 1 hour 35 minutes.

Tip: when a question mentions "how much more/less", "how long", "rounds to", or gives two quantities of the same type to compare, sketch a number line before reaching for a calculation — it usually shows straight away whether you need to add, subtract, or just read off a position.
