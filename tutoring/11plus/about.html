Absolute minimum standardised score for any CSSE offer: 303. Below this, no offer is made regardless of school (appeals and special considerations sit outside this rule).

How the Standardised Score Works

Raw English and Maths scores are each standardised to a mean of 100 and SD of 15, then combined 1:1 with an optional age adjustment for younger candidates:

standardised = ((raw − μ) ÷ σ) × 15 + 100
total = 1.5 × [(std. Maths + days-younger × Maths adj.) + (std. English + days-younger × English adj.)]

A candidate scoring exactly average on both papers, born on the base date, always totals 300.

The Age Adjustment: How Big Is It?

CSSE re-runs a statistical test every year to check whether younger candidates in the cohort are scoring systematically lower. If it's significant for a subject, that subject gets a small per-day boost for younger candidates; if not, the adjustment is zero for that subject that year. It's recalculated from scratch each year, not a fixed rule.

Rule of thumb: English is worth roughly half a point per month of age gap, fairly consistently across years. Maths is much less reliable — when it applies at all it's a quarter of a point per month or less, and in 3 of the last 8 years it was zero. Full-year effect (oldest vs youngest child in the same cohort, identical raw performance) has ranged from about 6 to 14 points — meaningful next to the 303 minimum, but not enough to overturn a large raw-score gap on its own.

† 2025 and 2026 rows use CSSE's own published factors. 2019–2024 are estimated by regressing raw score against birth month in the published raw-score files (only month of birth is available, not exact date, and CSSE's actual test isn't necessarily a simple linear regression). Where checkable against a published figure (2026), the estimate landed within about 1–4% of the real number — close enough to trust the shape of the trend, though the exact per-year values before 2025 aren't official CSSE figures.

What Does That Actually Take on the Papers?

The standardised score hides how hard the raw papers actually were that year — a mean/SD trend on its own doesn't tell you what percentage a candidate needed. Using the same method CSSE itself uses for its own rough guidance (assuming even performance across English and Maths), here's the raw percentage needed to clear the absolute minimum (303) and two illustrative school cut-offs, by year.

CSSE's own Information Guide gives rough rule-of-thumb percentages too: ~55–60% for the Southend priority area, ~65% for Southend/most schools outside priority area, ~65–70% for Colchester County High, ~70% for CRGS/KEGS priority area, ~75% for KEGS outside priority area. Those are stated as general guidance rather than tied to a specific year, so they sit a little below the figures above — cut-offs have crept up since that guidance was last set.

Method: assumes a candidate scored the same standardised z-score on both papers (CSSE's own "equal performance" assumption), ignores the small age adjustment, and assumes each paper is marked out of 60 — inferred from the raw-score files (Maths hits exactly 60 in nearly every year) rather than confirmed in any published document. Treat these as indicative, not exact.
